## Part 1 — The review, as delivered

Yes, there are current methods that fit, but none of them is the first thing to
do. The model has almost nothing left to learn on the problems we train and
test it on, and the training step has a fault I measured today. I'd also
withdraw my last suggestion (the five-iteration rehearsal): I now expect it to
cost pod-hours and show nothing moving.

Nothing in the repo changed; today's probes were scratch scripts, small and
unregistered.

### Critical overview

1. **The benchmark is used up.**
   - The starting network, with no search at all, is at par on 1,176 of 1,200
     suite problems. With search it solves all 1,200, and 588 of 600 deep
     scripted problems.
   - In M1, 7,792 of 8,000 self-play episodes ended exactly at par, so the loop
     trained almost entirely on its own successes.
   - The cause is that Phase 1 was supervised on depths 1–6, the same band the
     suites measure. The spec asked for easy strata only.

2. **The loop has almost no learning signal.**
   - On ring-0 the improved-policy target differs from the network's own policy
     on 57 of 1,305 rows.
   - The win/draw/loss value head never went live in M1's 20 iterations. Its
     switch needs 100 failures in the held-out slice, and the model rarely
     fails.

3. **The training step interferes with itself (measured today).**
   - At the starting network, the policy loss sends a gradient of 0.5 into the
     shared transformer layers; the steps and value losses send 2.6 and 3.1. On
     the stress ring (ring-V) it is 11 from policy against 44 from steps alone.
   - The steps head trains on every ring row, including unsolved episodes,
     where the "target" is how long the model wandered. It is within 0.06 steps
     on at-par rows and 7–11 steps off on the rest.
   - The win/draw/loss head starts untrained (loss 1.92, worse than uniform
     guessing at 1.10) and trains through the shared layers from step one.
   - This is the likely source of the "forgetting" the last three sweeps
     chased, and of the 104-hit loss in SWEEP-m3's control arm.

4. **A good value function already exists and the search ignores it (measured
   today).** On 180 suite states, picking the child with the lowest predicted
   steps-to-solve chooses an optimal action 179 times; the policy's top choice
   does so 178 times. The search reads only the untrained win/draw/loss head.

5. **Gate 10b counts ties as misses (measured today).** 51 of those 180 states
   have more than one optimal action. The policy is optimal on 98.9% of them,
   while the gate reports 97.0% agreement with BFS's single label. The 2-hit
   band sits inside tie-break noise, and SWEEP-m3's "+19" may be tie-breaks.

6. **Each question is expensive.** It currently costs a registered page and a
   pod run, with 8–15 minutes per arm on CPU.

### Methods that fit

None of these has been tested here; the evidence is from other domains.

| # | Method | What it would do here | Where it has worked |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cost-to-go value with bootstrapped targets (DeepCubeA) | Use steps-to-solve as the search's value, trained by one-step lookahead through the exact rules, so no failed episodes are needed | Rubik's cube, sliding puzzles |
| 2 | Classification loss for the steps head (HL-Gauss) | Predict a distribution over step counts: bounded loss scale, and P(at par) falls out of it | Atari, chess without search |
| 3 | Head warm-up before fine-tuning (LP-FT) | Train new heads with the shared layers frozen, then unfreeze | Vision transfer benchmarks |
| 4 | Policy-surprise weighting (KataGo) | Sample ring rows by how far the target is from the prior | Go |
| 5 | Reanalyse | Recompute old rows' targets with the current network | MuZero family |
| 6 | Regret-prioritised problems, then ACCEL | Replay and mutate problems the model fails: the Phase-3 generator in its simplest form | Maze and game curricula |
| 7 | Self-competition in the search (GAZ PTP), or "sample, keep the best, imitate" (Gumbeldore) | Published forms of the par game; the second needs no value head | Routing, job-shop scheduling |
| 8 | Hindsight relabelling and test-time variants (Minimo, AlphaProof) | Turn failed searches into solved training problems | Formal mathematics |

### Order I'd do it in

1. **Restore headroom.** Train weaker starting networks (depth ≤ 2 or ≤ 3 only)
   and make the loop extend them to depths 4–6, where BFS grades every move
   exactly. Score "is the chosen action optimal" instead of single-label
   agreement.
2. **Repair the training step.** Stop training steps on unsolved episodes,
   apply methods 2 and 3, and log each loss and its gradient.
3. **Let the search use the steps head** (method 1), as a one-lever round.
4. **Then methods 4–5, then 6.** Methods 7–8 are larger bets.

An external yardstick also exists: the CommonCore equations benchmark, where
ConPoLe and an August 2026 paper report about 93%. I have only read that
paper's abstract page.

### Decisions for you

- **Step 3 touches a settled decision.** The spec fixed the value head as
  win/draw/loss against par; step 3 keeps the par game but changes which head
  the search trusts, so it needs your ruling.
- **Step 1 changes what Phase 1 covers.** That makes it a new registered round;
  the current network stays as the reference.
- **An exploration lane.** I'd allow cheap unregistered runs to find effects,
  with registered runs to confirm them.
- **A write-up in the repo.** I can commit this review and its probe scripts as
  a dated page if you want the numbers reproducible.

## Amendment (2026-11-15, evening) — Exploration (not evidence): point 5 re-read on the gate's own states

Part 1's point 5 was read off 180 suite **start** states. Gate 10b's own
population is different: 1,229 held-out states along the BFS paths of problems
of depth ≤ 3. `scripts/explore_optimal_gate.py` (AGENTS §7.1) labels every
legal action of those 1,229 states by exact BFS and scores the anchor against
the optimal set.

| reading, gate 10b's 1,229 states, the Phase-1 anchor | result |
|---|---:|
| states with more than one optimal action | 267 |
| hits against BFS's single label (reproduces the gate) | 1,192 |
| hits against the optimal set | 1,198 |
| the gate's 37 misses: an optimal action other than BFS's / a wrong action | 6 / 31 |

**What changes in point 5.**
- "The policy is optimal on 98.9% of them" was true of the 180 start states. On
  the gate's own states it is 97.5% (1,198 of 1,229), against the gate's 97.0%.
  The gate understates by 6 hits, not by the margin point 5 implied.
- **"SWEEP-m3's '+19' may be tie-breaks" is withdrawn.** At most 6 of the gate's
  misses are ties, so at least 13 of the 19 hits gained there were wrong
  actions becoming right ones.
- What stands: 31 of 1,229 top actions are wrong at the anchor, a single-label
  gate cannot tell a tie from an error, and a 2-hit band is narrower than the
  6-hit tie effect.

This reading is exact arithmetic over a frozen population, but it was not
registered, so it is recorded here as an exploration. A registered instrument
that scores against optimal sets is part of the round that follows the
weak-start exploration.
